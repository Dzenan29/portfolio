About

I'm Dzenan. I work where hardware, software and infrastructure meet.

Since November 2022 I've been part of the IT side of an organic chemistry research group at Ruhr-Universität Bochum. The work is wonderfully broad: one day it's a Proxmox host or a GlassFish thread dump, the next it's an ESP32 with a camera pointed at a pressure gauge.

My path wasn't a straight line. I started in IT support and system administration in Italy, moved to Germany, learned the language and built websites before landing in research IT. What connects all of it is curiosity about how systems actually work - down to the signal on the wire.

In practice that means: when a gauge can only be read by walking past it, I build a reader. When a service fails at night, I want to know why. And when something works, I document it so it keeps working without me.

How I approach engineering

  1. 01

    Understand before building

    The best solution often starts with a better question.

  2. 02

    Simple beats clever

    A system someone else can maintain is worth more than an elegant one nobody understands.

  3. 03

    Measure, don't assume

    Logs, numbers and tests before opinions - and honesty about what wasn't measured.

  4. 04

    Leave it documented

    Runbooks and wikis turn individual knowledge into team knowledge.
